Why officers stall

You cannot prepare for a job nobody has described to you.

Every aspiring officer we meet is willing. Almost none of them can tell you what they will actually be doing on watch, in port, or during an inspection — because no one has ever spelled it out. That gap is not a knowledge problem. It is a mentorship problem.

The books teach the rules

The ship runs on routine. Knowing Rule 15 is not the same as a crossing situation at 0300 with a fishing fleet on the bow and the Master asleep.

Your rank does not decide your work

One ship puts you on safety gear and port documents. The next hands you the passage plan. The job description changes with the company.

You find out too late

Most officers learn what they were missing during their first month aboard — the most expensive place in the world to learn it.

Why four, and not just your rank

A Third Officer's responsibilities are not guaranteed. On one ship you are safety equipment and port documents. On the next, the Master hands you the passage plan, or you cover the Second Officer's charts and cargo watch. Sell a man one rank and you have prepared him for a job he might not be given. So the club covers the work you might actually be handed — Third Officer and Second Officer, plus passage planning and the COLREGs underneath both.

The honest part

Why is it only ₱6,500?

It is a fair question. A Third Officer in international trade earns around ₱180,000 a month. Against that, the price of this club is roughly a single day of the rank it is preparing you for — and most courses that promise far less ask for far more.

We did not build this to get rich

We were seafarers first. We came up under seniors who guarded what they knew and made you feel stupid for asking — eager to learn, with nobody willing to teach. Nothing about that was a knowledge shortage. It was a kindness shortage.

So we made what we wish we had

Every lesson here is the explanation we needed and never got, recorded by officers who remember exactly how it felt not to know. The price is set to be reachable by a cadet's allowance, not to squeeze a rank out of you.

You are not on your own

Ask the basic question. Nobody here will laugh.

The thing that stops most juniors is not the difficulty of the work — it is the fear of looking stupid in front of the people they need help from. So the club has one rule: no question is too basic.

The members' group chat

Every member is in it. Ask anything — how to fill a form, what the Chief Mate meant, what happens at your first PSC inspection — and get an answer from someone who was standing where you are last contract. No judgement, no gatekeeping, no made-to-feel-small.

The maritime file library

Forms, checklists, reporting templates and references — contributed by the members themselves, ship after ship, because everyone in here has agreed to help the next man succeed. It grows every month, and it is the paperwork a junior officer is expected to produce and has never been shown.

Questions

Before you join

I am still a cadet / rating. Is this too advanced?

No — this is exactly who it is built for. The whole point is showing you the job before you are standing in it, so you arrive knowing what is expected instead of finding out during your first month.

Can I study offline?

Yes. Download the videos in port on the Android app and watch them at sea with no connection. The videos play in the app; on an iPhone they play in Safari at app.seamanapp.com. Notes, files and chat are on the web everywhere.

Is it a subscription?

No. ₱6,500 once, and the club is yours for life — including lessons added later.

How do I pay?

Scan a QR Ph code with GCash, Maya or any participating bank app. Access opens automatically, usually within a minute. If you would rather send the payment to a person, message us on Facebook and we will enrol you by hand.

Does this give me a certificate or STCW endorsement?

No, and we will not pretend otherwise. This is peer mentorship from serving officers — knowledge and preparation, not approved or assessed training. It confers no certificate and no endorsement.

What if it is not for me?

Open the chapters above and read every lesson title, then read the reviews on our Facebook page from members who went through it. If it still does not look like what you need, do not join.
