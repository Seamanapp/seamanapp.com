Why this exists

You were taught the rules. You were never shown the watch.
The exams assume a conversation with a senior officer that, for most of us, never happened.

Nobody hands you a written procedure for how to take over a watch at 0345 in traffic, what to check before you accept the con, or what the Master actually wants to be called for. You learn it by being lucky with your seniors — or you learn it the hard way. This course is that conversation, in the order a voyage really happens.

Not another rulebook

COLREGs and SOLAS are on your ship already. What is missing is what a competent officer does with them at 0345 in traffic.

Good to know

How much of the course is finished?

Sixteen lessons — about two hours and twenty minutes — are complete and watchable the moment you enrol: the introduction, the whole Foundations chapter, and five of the nine lessons on the bridge and its instruments. The other sixteen chapters are in production and appear in your account as each one is finished, at no extra cost. That unfinished state is the reason the price is ₱3,250 rather than ₱17,000.

When will the rest be released?

Chapters are filmed and released in voyage order, and we publish each one the week it is finished rather than promising a date we might miss. Your two years of access begins the day you enrol and comfortably covers the build.

Is this a certificate or STCW course?

No. It's honest peer-to-peer mentorship from a serving officer. It confers no certificate and no endorsement, and it doesn't replace your company's SMS or the officer teaching you onboard. It's the conversation the certificates assume you already had.

How long do I keep access?

Two years from the day you enrol — one payment, no subscription. Everything released in that window is yours to watch and re-watch as often as you like.

Can I study offline?

Yes. Download the videos in port on the Android app and watch them mid-ocean. The whole app is built offline-first for a ship's connection.

Who is it for?

Entry to intermediate — cadets, new OOWs, and anyone who knows the theory but wants to know how the watch actually runs. There are dedicated lanes for bulk, tanker and container ships.
