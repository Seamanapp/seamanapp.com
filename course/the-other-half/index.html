The Other Half

A Third Officer's job, outside the watch — one ship, one month, in the order it actually happens.

Every third officer is trained to keep a watch. Almost none of them are trained for the rest of the day. The rest of the day is the port documents, the crew change, the muster list, the lifeboat inventory, the drill report, the requisition, the bonded store, and the inspector standing at the top of the gangway. It is most of the working week, it is what gets a ship detained, and it is the part your name ends up attached to.

Why this exists

You were trained to keep the watch.
Nobody trained you for the rest of the day — and the rest of the day is what gets a ship detained.

🗂️

The real forms, on screen

Port declarations, the muster list, the drill report, the requisition, the bonded-store account — filled in the way they are actually filled in, with the reason behind each line.

📅

One ship, one month

A handover in Singapore, a crew change the same week, a month end at sea, a port call and an inspector at the gangway — in the order they happen, not a syllabus sorted by subject.

✍️

You do it, nobody marks it

Every lesson ends with something real to do on your own ship, photographed by you. Where a shortcut exists you are told what it costs and who has to approve it.

Who is teaching

A working officer who did the job, not a retired lecturer.

Someone who carried the third officer's file this contract — who knows which document the agent asks for first, what the flag inspector opens, which requisition comes back wrong and why, and what the surveyor is really looking at when he asks to see the lifeboat inventory.

Simplified Maritime has taught Filipino officers on Facebook for years, and the Navigators Club community is now more than 1,900 seafarers. The Other Half is that same voice, written down properly and filmed.

Is this a certificate or STCW course?

No. It's peer-to-peer mentorship from a working officer. It confers no certificate and no endorsement, nothing is marked, and it doesn't replace your company's safety-management system. Who is it for?

New and soon-to-be third officers, and cadets who want to know what the job is before they hold it. Second officers who never had this handover will recognise most of it.
